Beginner Guide

A condensed version of the PRTS beginner guide — the resources, operators, stages and base decisions that actually change your first month. Condensed and translated from prts.wiki.

What kind of game is this

Three layers at once, and all three matter:

  • Tower defence — you place operators on tiles, they block enemies, and enemies walk a fixed route into them. Block count, deployment cost and attack interval decide most fights before anyone swings.
  • Roster building — you raise a pool of operators over months. Levels, promotions, skills and modules are the long game.
  • Story — the campaign is the main way the world is told, and it unlocks almost every system in the game.

A squad is up to 12 of your own operators plus 1 support unit borrowed from another player.

The four resources

  • Sanity — the stamina bar. A stage costs 6–36, and you regenerate 1 every 6 minutes up to a cap. Almost every upgrade material in the game comes from spending sanity, so how you spend it is the real long-term decision.
  • Originite Prime — the paid currency and the scarcest thing you own. Use it on Pro Packs first (they return the same orundum plus materials); pulling with raw primes early is the classic beginner mistake. You need 193 primes to buy every Pro Pack, so don't waste them on furniture.
  • Orundum — the pull currency: 600 per pull, 6000 per ten-pull. Daily and weekly missions plus Annihilation give roughly 3000 a week, enough for about three ten-pulls a month.
  • LMD — ordinary money, spent on levels, promotions, modules and crafting. Trading posts in the base are the main income; don't farm the main story for it.

Building a roster

  • You start with four operators for free: Amiya, Rangers, Noir Corne and Yato.
  • Headhunting — 2% for a 6★, 8% for a 5★, 50% for a 4★, 40% for a 3★. The first ten pulls on a new banner always contain a 5★ or better. After 50 pulls without a 6★, every pull adds 2% until one appears.
  • Recruitment — pick up to three of five tags and wait, up to 9 hours. Only a full 9 hours protects the Senior Operator and Top Operator tags from being crossed out, so always set 9:00 when you see them. Never press "Stop Recruitment" — it cancels the recruitment instead of finishing it.
  • Rarity is not everything. 3★ and 4★ operators are cheap to raise and can clear the whole main story. A 4★ with the right kit often does a job no 6★ you own can do.

The stats that decide fights

  • HP, ATK, DEF, RES — HP is the damage one operator can absorb before being forced to retreat. ATK is the damage of one hit, DEF subtracts from physical hits, RES cuts a percentage of Arts damage.
  • The two formulas — physical damage is ATK − DEF; Arts damage is ATK × (1 − RES%). A hit always does at least 5% of the attacker's ATK, so stacking DEF never makes you truly immune.
  • DP cost — what you pay to deploy. Retreating refunds half; redeploying the same operator costs 1.5×, then 2×. The cap is 99 DP, so a botched retreat can lock you out of the field.
  • Redeploy time — from about 16–18 seconds for fast-redeploy specialists to 70 seconds for most operators. Knowing which of yours is fast is what makes emergency blocking possible.
  • Block count — how many enemies one operator can hold. Once it is full, further enemies walk straight past.

Raising an operator

Six things to upgrade, in the order they usually matter:

  • Level — battle records plus LMD. Cheap at low levels, very expensive near the cap of each promotion stage.
  • Promotion — once an operator hits the level cap, promoting resets the level to 1 but keeps the stats, raises the cap, and widens the attack range. The second promotion unlocks modules.
  • Skills — levels up to 7 are shared by all of an operator's skills and are usually worth more than raw levels. Mastery beyond 7 needs the second promotion, and each skill is mastered separately and only one can be equipped.
  • Potential — duplicates give tokens. It is a small bonus on most operators; do not chase it.
  • Modules — a small stat bump plus a change to the class branch and talents. Skip them until you already have several promoted operators; promoting more people is worth more early.
  • Trust — builds up passively from clearing stages. The stat bonus is complete at 100%, so just keep using the operator.

The eight classes

  • Vanguard — cheap, generates DP, holds the first thirty seconds of a stage.
  • Guard — melee damage, the largest class, and the one that usually decides whether a lane holds.
  • Defender — high HP and DEF, blocks two or three enemies, keeps everything else alive.
  • Sniper — ranged physical damage on high ground, the usual answer to drones.
  • Caster — ranged Arts damage, the answer to armour.
  • Medic — heals; without one, a line collapses no matter how much damage you have.
  • Supporter — slows, buffs, debuffs and summons. The class you learn to love later.
  • Specialist — push, pull, fast redeploy, traps. The class that solves the map nobody else can.

Within each class, the branch is what actually matters — two Snipers can behave nothing alike. Read the branch before you invest.

Running a stage

  • Read the intel panel first — the map layout, the enemy list and the stage mechanics are all there before you spend sanity. Most wipes are a mechanic you did not read.
  • Three stars means every target enemy killed and no life lost. Anything with at least one life left still counts as a clear, but only three stars drops the first-clear reward.
  • Failures now refund sanity — since the 2025 summer update, a failed or abandoned run gives the sanity back, so experimenting is cheap.
  • Drills are free and unlimited — practise a stage as many times as you like before committing.
  • Auto Deploy is not a guarantee — it replays your last three-star clear, but raising an operator or a random talent can make it fail. A clear using a support unit cannot be auto-deployed at all.

Where to spend sanity

  • Push the main story first. It unlocks the base, resource stages, events and every other system, so progress is worth more than perfecting one squad.
  • Resource stages — LS for battle records, CE for LMD, SK for base materials, CA for skill books, AP for certificates, PR for chips. LS and CE are open every day; the rest rotate on fixed weekdays, so plan around them.
  • Annihilation — the weekly orundum payout, capped at 1200 and raised to 1800 as you clear the permanent maps. Clear them without a support unit so the cap stays unlocked.
  • Event stages — the last few stages of a side story drop tier-3 materials far more often than the main story. If an event is running, that is where your sanity goes.
  • Chips and chips — promoting a 5★ or 6★ needs chip packs and a chip catalyst, and the catalyst only comes from red certificates. 4★ promotion does not need one, so don't buy them early.

The base

  • Unlocks after stage 0-11 and pays for itself many times over. Build it early rather than treating it as a side activity.
  • Left side: production. Power plants feed everything, factories make battle records and pure gold, trading posts turn gold into LMD. The classic layouts are 2 power / 5 factory / 2 trading, or the calmer 3/4/2.
  • Right side: functions. Reception room, workshop, office and training room cannot be downgraded, so upgrade them when you can afford to.
  • Base skills and morale — operators have base skills and lose morale while working. Put them in the rooms that match, and rotate them into dorms before morale hits zero, or the skill stops working.
  • Do not promote operators just for base skills. The payback takes years; promote for combat first.

Certificates and shops

  • Green (Qualification) — from 4★ and below. Spend the first tier every month, then the tier-3 materials.
  • Yellow (Distinction) — from 5★ and above, and the most valuable currency you have. Save for a 6★ contract, or for the 258-certificate bundle of 38 pulls — never buy only the first stages of it.
  • Blue (Commendation) — headhunting-only. Useful to fill gaps early, but never pour orundum into the middle banner just to earn it.
  • Red (Purchase) — from the AP stage. Chip catalysts and the shop-exclusive operators are the only things worth buying.
  • Purple (Intel) — from rerun compensation, and slow to earn. Orundum is the best value in that shop.

After the beginning

  • Read the tutorial pop-ups marked with an i. They explain every new system faster than any guide.
  • Search before you ask — the wiki has a page for every mechanic, material and stage, and the in-game data is exact.
  • Copying a clear is fine, but read why it works; the point is to stop needing the video.
  • Terminology is worth learning. Knowing what "block", "redeploy" and "Arts" mean is most of the skill.

Against catastrophe, we are not helpless.
